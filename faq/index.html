Cost and insurance

Is the consultation really free?

It is. You'll get an exam, our honest opinion on whether you need treatment, and what it would cost. If the answer is that you don't need anything yet, we'll tell you that.

Do you take my insurance?

We work directly with most major providers. If you want to know about your specific plan before you come in, call or text us the name of it and we'll look into it.

How do payments work?

A down payment to start, then monthly payments set to fit your budget. You'll know the full number before anything begins, so there's nothing to discover partway through.

Treatment

Do you treat adults, or just kids and teens?

Adults too, and more of them than people expect. Treatment works at any age.

Invisalign or Angel Aligner: what's the difference?

We're certified Invisalign providers and we also offer Angel Aligner. Which one fits you depends on your bite and what needs moving, so we'd rather walk you through it at the consultation than guess here.

Something came loose. Do I need to come in right away?

Call us. A loose wire or bracket shouldn't sit for weeks, and we make room for urgent visits quickly. If it's after hours and it's uncomfortable rather than painful, it can usually wait until morning.

Everyday care

How do I brush with braces?

Brush after every meal, not just morning and night. Food catches around brackets in a way it never did before, and the spots that get missed are right at the gumline and just under the wire. Angle the brush toward your gums, then again toward the biting edge, so you clean above and below each bracket. Two minutes, every time.

Do I need special tools?

A regular soft toothbrush still does most of the work. What helps most is a small interdental brush, sometimes called a proxy brush, for getting under the wire where a normal brush cannot reach. We'll show you what we recommend at your bonding appointment, so you aren't guessing in the toothpaste aisle.

How do I floss with braces on?

Floss threaders let you get the floss up behind the wire. It's fiddly for about a week and quick after that. If you're finding it impossible on certain teeth, tell us at your next visit and we'll work through it with you.

My gums bleed when I brush. Is that normal?

Early on, usually yes. It's the gums reacting to plaque sitting along the bracket line, and the instinct to brush there more gently is the wrong one. Brush that area carefully but thoroughly and it generally settles within a week or two. If it's still bleeding after that, or your gums are puffy and sore, let us know.

What happens if I don't keep up with it?

This is the one worth reading. Plaque left sitting around a bracket pulls minerals out of the enamel and leaves a chalky white square behind. When the braces come off, those marks stay. Straight teeth with white scars on them is not the result anyone wants, and it is entirely preventable with brushing.

What can't I eat?

Anything hard, sticky, chewy or crunchy enough to pull a bracket off or bend a wire. The usual suspects are ice, hard candy, caramel, taffy, gum, popcorn, nuts and hard rolls. Crunchy things like apples and raw carrots are fine if you cut them up first, and corn is fine off the cob. We'll go through the full list with you when the braces go on.

My teeth ache after an adjustment. What helps?

Soreness for two or three days after braces go on, and after each adjustment, is normal and expected. Soft food helps: pasta, eggs, yogurt, soup, smoothies. A warm salt water rinse helps. Whatever pain reliever you'd normally reach for is fine. If it's still sore after about a week, or one specific tooth is sharply painful rather than generally achy, call us.

Can I still play sports?

Yes, and plenty of our patients do. Wear a mouthguard. The ones designed to fit over braces are different from the boil and bite kind, so ask us which to get rather than picking one off the shelf.

Do I still need to see my family dentist?

Yes, and keep going. We handle moving your teeth; your dentist handles cleanings, cavities and everything else. Braces make those cleanings more important rather than less, so don't put them off while you're in treatment. If you think you might need to go more often than usual, ask us and we'll tell you what we're seeing.
