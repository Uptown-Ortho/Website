Getting started

What age should my child first see an orthodontist?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first check by age 7. Most kids that age won't need treatment yet, but an early look lets us spot bite or jaw issues while they're easier to guide. The visit is free, and if it's too early, we'll simply check again later.

Who are your orthodontists?

Dr. Brandan LeBourdais and Dr. Hannah Collison are orthodontists, dentists who completed 2 more years of specialty training in straightening teeth and aligning bites. Both are members of the American Association of Orthodontists.

Is Uptown Orthodontics family-owned?

Yes. Uptown is a family-owned practice led by our orthodontists, Dr. Brandan LeBourdais and Dr. Hannah Collison, and has served Bay City for more than 50 years. Every treatment decision is made by the doctor who examines you, and you'll see the same doctors from your first visit to the day your braces come off.

What's the difference between a family-owned practice and a corporate-owned one?

Many orthodontic offices are now owned or managed by large national groups. At a family-owned practice like ours, our orthodontists set the treatment plan, the schedule and the pricing, with no outside company involved. You see the same doctors and team at every visit, and our decisions stay focused on your care and your family's budget.

Do I need a referral from my dentist?

No. Call or text us and we'll get you on the schedule. Plenty of our patients find us on their own.

What does the first visit cost?

Nothing. The exam and the evaluation are free. If we need 3D imaging to look at something specific, that's no extra charge either.

Cost and insurance

Is the consultation really free?

It is. You'll get an exam, our honest opinion on whether you need treatment, and what it would cost. If the answer is that you don't need anything yet, we'll tell you that.

Do you take my insurance?

Yes. We work directly with most major insurances (Delta Dental, Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan, MetLife, Cigna, and more). We file claims for you and apply your orthodontic benefit directly to your treatment. Text your plan name to 989.894.2929 and we'll confirm your coverage before your free consultation.

Does insurance cover braces? Can I use my HSA or FSA?

Many dental plans include an orthodontic benefit, usually for patients under 19, and some plans cover adults. HSA and FSA funds can be used for orthodontic treatment. We check your benefits, file claims for you and build your coverage into your payment plan.

How much do braces or aligners cost?

Every smile is different, so your cost is based on your personal treatment plan. At your free consultation we map out the full scope of treatment, then set you up with a down payment and flexible monthly payments that fit your family. We're also happy to help you make the most of your insurance benefits. Give us a call and we'll walk you through the numbers.

How do payments work?

Most families start with a down payment and pay monthly through our interest-free in-house plans. You'll see the full cost, your insurance share and your monthly before anything begins. HSA and FSA funds can be used too.

Treatment

Do you treat adults, or just kids and teens?

Yes, we treat adults. Treatment works at any age as long as your teeth and gums are healthy. Many adults choose clear aligners or tooth-colored braces, and many adult cases finish in 12 to 18 months. Your free consultation covers your options.

Angel Aligners and Invisalign®: what's the difference?

Angel Aligners and Invisalign® both use clear, custom-made aligners to straighten teeth comfortably and discreetly, with very similar treatment experiences and results. We proudly offer both options and remain an Invisalign® provider, allowing us to recommend the aligner system that best fits your individual needs.

Something came loose. Do I need to come in right away?

Call us. A loose wire or bracket shouldn't sit for weeks, and we make room for urgent visits quickly. If it's after hours and it's uncomfortable rather than painful, it can usually wait until morning.

Everyday care

How do I brush with braces?

Brush after every meal, not just morning and night. Food catches around brackets in a way it never did before, and the spots that get missed are right at the gumline and just under the wire. Angle the brush toward your gums, then again toward the biting edge, so you clean above and below each bracket. Two minutes, every time.

Do I need special tools?

A regular soft toothbrush still does most of the work. What helps most is a small interdental brush, sometimes called a proxy brush, for getting under the wire where a normal brush cannot reach. We'll show you what we recommend at your bonding appointment, so you aren't guessing in the toothpaste aisle.

How do I floss with braces?

Floss threaders let you get the floss up behind the wire. It can feel a little tricky at first, but most patients get the hang of it quickly. We also offer and show you how to use Platypus® flossers, which make flossing around braces faster and easier. If you're struggling using either, tell us at your next visit and we'll work through it with you.

My gums bleed when I brush. Is that normal?

Early on, usually yes. It's the gums reacting to plaque sitting along the bracket line, and the instinct to brush there more gently is the wrong one. Brush that area carefully but thoroughly and it generally settles within a week or two. If it's still bleeding after that, or your gums are puffy and sore, let us know.

What happens if I don't keep up with it?

This is the one worth reading. Plaque left sitting around a bracket pulls minerals out of the enamel and leaves a chalky white square behind. When the braces come off, those marks stay. Straight teeth with white scars on them is not the result anyone wants, and it is entirely preventable with brushing.

What can't I eat?

Anything hard, sticky, chewy or crunchy enough to pull a bracket off or bend a wire. The usual suspects are ice, hard candy, caramel, taffy, gum, popcorn, nuts and hard rolls. Crunchy things like apples and raw carrots are fine if you cut them up first, and corn is fine off the cob. We'll go through the full list with you when the braces go on.

My teeth ache after an adjustment. What helps?

Soreness for two or three days after braces go on, and after each adjustment, is normal and expected. Soft food helps: pasta, eggs, yogurt, soup, smoothies. A warm salt water rinse helps. Whatever pain reliever you'd normally reach for is fine. If it's still sore after about a week, or one specific tooth is sharply painful rather than generally achy, call us.

Can I still play sports?

Yes, and plenty of our patients do. Wear a mouthguard. The ones designed to fit over braces are different from the boil and bite kind, so ask us which to get rather than picking one off the shelf.

Do I still need to see my family dentist?

Yes, and keep going. We handle moving your teeth; your dentist handles cleanings, cavities and everything else. Braces make those cleanings more important rather than less, so don't put them off while you're in treatment. If you think you might need to go more often than usual, ask us and we'll tell you what we're seeing.
